Hello team,

I have drafted the participant emails for the [Festival Year] Festival. Each attached template names its audience and send condition. The lifecycle map shows which sends exist in PafeTools and which would be new Festival behavior.

The proposed flow is:

1. When membership registration opens, invite prior-year teachers and accompanists. Send a welcome after a current-year membership is confirmed.
2. Shopify sends the order receipt to the purchasing parent. Festival does not send a second receipt. After registration is validated, Festival sends the parent either a confirmation or a specific action-required notice.
3. Parents receive timely waitlist status, invitation, promotion, withdrawal, and no-room notices as those decisions are recorded and released. Routine student registration and repertoire changes are grouped into separate weekly summaries for each teacher and accompanist.
4. When staff approves and publishes the final schedule, send each family its student schedule and repertoire, and each teacher and accompanist a role-specific schedule of their assigned students. Send a correction only to people affected by a later published change.
5. Under the proposed Festival policy, when staff releases placement results, send a placement notice to the family. Legacy placement recipients cannot be verified from the checkout because its runtime template controls To/Cc; teacher and accompanist handling needs a policy decision.

The weekly summaries are proposed Festival behavior, not a PafeTools feature. They should include only changes relevant to the named member and should be skipped if there are no changes. Schedule emails should be staff-triggered after review, because they contain individual times, rooms, and repertoire.

Please review the audience and trigger matrix in LIFECYCLE.md, then the individual .txt templates. Fields in [brackets] need real values or approved wording before any send. The legacy template text is not available in this checkout, so these are new drafts informed by the C# send paths rather than transcriptions.
